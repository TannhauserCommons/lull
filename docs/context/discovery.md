# Discovery: sources, limits and how the feed is built

Owner: Discovery session (`crates/discovery`). Source choice and why: [ADR-0030](../adr/0030-discovery-sources.md). Taste model: [taste.md](taste.md).

## Sources and their limits

| Source | Used for | Limit lull keeps | Cached | Auth |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ListenBrainz `api.listenbrainz.org/1` | fresh releases, CF recommendations, tag radio, artist radio, recording metadata | 1 call/s (server allows 30 per 10 s per IP; 429 beyond) | fresh releases 6 h; radio and recommendations 1 day; recording metadata 30 days | none; `LULL_LISTENBRAINZ_TOKEN` sent when set; `LULL_LISTENBRAINZ_USER` enables recommendations |
| ListenBrainz labs `labs.api.listenbrainz.org` | similar artists | same pace | 7 days | none |
| MusicBrainz `musicbrainz.org/ws/2` | finding artists and release groups by name, discographies (with credits), tracklists, tags, an artist's links to its iTunes and Deezer pages (`inc=url-rels`, with its tags in the same call), tag + date search | 1.1 s between calls (1/s per IP average; going over gets every call a 503). Other lull crates share the IP | lookups and artist links 30 days (a release group not found: 1 day, as new releases reach MusicBrainz after the catalogs); discographies 7 days; tag searches 12 h | none; User-Agent `lull/<ver> ( repo URL )` from `lull_core::http` |
| Last.fm `ws.audioscrobbler.com/2.0` | artist bio and listeners, similar artists and top tracks with play counts (artist pages), tag description, tag top artists, similar tags; `album.getInfo`, the first place an album's cover is looked up by name ([Pictures](#pictures)) | 0.5 s between calls, lull-wide (`http::LASTFM`: scrobbling and history imports share it; "several per second" risks suspension) | 7 days (tag text 30); top tracks 1 day; album covers 30 days | `LULL_LASTFM_API_KEY` or the built-in key; off without one |

A quota error arrives as HTTP 200 with `error.code` 4: the window closes for 5 s and the call is tried once more | searches 10 min; tracklists and artists 7 days; an artist's albums and top 1 day | none |
| iTunes Search `itunes.apple.com/search` | catalog search where Deezer is closed: per query an `entity=song` call (25; their albums come along) and an `entity=album` call (25) side by side, store `LULL_CATALOG_COUNTRY` (default US); `lookup?id=<collectionId>&entity=song` for the album page of an album it found; `entity=song` or `entity=album` (10) for [share links](#share-links); for artist pages `entity=musicArtist` (10) and the artist's `lookup?id=<artistId>&entity=album` and `entity=song` (200 each); `entity=album` (5) for a cover Last.fm hasn't, from what searches leave spare ([Pictures](#pictures)) | about 20 calls a minute (Apple's guidance); lull spends at most 18 per rolling minute, then searches without it. Work nobody waits on (artist catalogs, share links, covers, imports) only while 6 of them stay for searches | searches 10 min; tracklists 7 days; artists by name and album covers 30 days; an artist's lookups 1 day | none |
| Wikidata `query.wikidata.org/sparql` | artist photos on Wikimedia Commons (P18) and iTunes and Deezer ids, by MBID (P434), every artist of a page in one query | 1 query/s (the service limits processing time per minute) | 30 days per MBID | none; lull's User-Agent |
| Cover Art Archive `coverartarchive.org` | covers of albums with a release-group MBID that Last.fm and iTunes haven't (`front-250/500/1200`) | none published; at most 4 requests at once (`Http::host_place`). Slow from the homelab: 3 s a 250 px thumbnail (median, 2026-11-04) | pictures 30 days | none |

- **Pacing** goes through `Http::pace`, shared with every lull crate. MusicBrainz uses core's `MUSICBRAINZ_PACE`, because library calls it from the same IP.
- **MusicBrainz, ListenBrainz, Last.fm and Wikidata are bounded** (`sources::Breaker`): each call times out after 5 s (`Http` retries a timeout, so about 17 s at most). After two failed calls in a row the service is left alone for a minute: calls fail at once with "isn't answering". A call abandoned before it answered (a radio or search out of time) counts as failed.
- **Search sources are capped at 8 s:** one that hasn't answered by then is named in `errors`, so every run ends with `search.more { done: true }`.
- **Caching:** rows unused for 90 days are pruned at start-up. When a source fails, the expired answer is served (`sources/cache.rs`).
- **Switches (Settings > Discovery):** collection's `settings` event carries `discovery.on.{listenbrainz,musicbrainz,lastfm}`. A source switched off is not used at all, cached answers included. Its calls fail with "switched off in Settings > Discovery", which shows up in `errors`, and the feed rebuilds.
- **Adventurousness:** `discovery.adventurousness` from the same event is radio's `steer` when the UI sends none (0.4 until then).
- Tokens that ListenBrainz gates (`/explore/lb-radio`, `/popularity/*`) aren't needed: top tracks come from artist radio with no similar artists.
- MusicBrainz tag + date search uses Lucene ranges: `tag:"x" AND firstreleasedate:[2026-08-02 TO *]`.

## The feed (`GET /api/feed`)

Built in the background; the first build happens while the first caller waits (tens of seconds, paced). Then:
- kept in memory and in `discovery_state` (served after a restart);
- rebuilt when six hours old, when follows change, after feedback or a taste reset, and on the listener's Refresh (`POST /api/feed/refresh`, at most hourly, a whole new shelf);
- hidden artists are filtered at serve time.

| Shelf | From |
|---|---|
| `newReleases` | ListenBrainz fresh releases (30 days, every size). Ranked: followed artists, then liked artists (taste), then releases tagged with followed tags or tags dug into (`Taste::dig_tags`: specific, on 2+ liked artists; albums and EPs above singles). Never a release the listener has met (a known song of it, or the song a single is named after) or a reissue ("remastered", "reissue" in the title). Only what can play: of the best 30, the 12 best that are saved or that Soulseek peers share (`feed::check_shelf`). Remembered answers (`Transfers::album_known`) fill the shelf at once; then `album_available` searches one release at a time, best first, and never for one ranked below a full shelf. The build waits 3 s for answers; later ones re-serve the shelf and announce `feed.updated`. A release without an answer is never shown, so the shelf is empty rather than wrong while Soulseek can't say; those are asked again every 5 minutes (free while Soulseek is offline or its checks wait). A stored feed loaded after a restart is checked again |
| `picks` | Stable at the core, fresh at the edge ([ADR-0100](../adr/0100-picks-stable-at-the-core-fresh-at-the-edge.md), `picking.rs`): the 3 strongest established artists and 2 strongest tags always seed, 1 more artist and up to 2 more tags are drawn from the next ones (to the 12th); about a fifth of the shelf comes from the edge (3 for a listener of few tags, 2 for a broad one). One track per artist, originals before other takes (`radio::is_version`). Nothing already played or liked (on any release), nothing by hidden or held-back artists, nothing the last 3 shelves showed as new. Except fresh releases, no artist the listener already likes. An automatic rebuild keeps up to half the last shelf's picks not taken (a pick gives way after 3 shelves); Refresh keeps none. How each seed's picks fared moves its weight between half and twice (`store::seed_outcomes`: heard +0.5, heard again on a later day +2, liked or saved +2, skipped −0.5, shrunk by n/(n+3)). 12. |
| `radioStarters` | Top artist, followed tags, the strongest tag dug into that isn't followed, the track heard 3+ times this week; on a cold start, three cold-start tags |
| `jumpBackIn` | Albums heard in the last 30 days, most recent first (5), then the month's most played artist that lull knows by its exact [ids](#artist-identity) (of the 5 most played; a credit like "A feat. B" holds none of its own). *Heard* is the taste model's play: to the end or past half; a play that ended at once isn't one |
| `followedTags` | `follows` at serve time. Each comes as a tag entity (`entities.tags`) with `related`: the specific tags on 2+ of this month's fresh releases that carry it, most often first, never a followed one (4; `feed::related_tags`, from the releases the feed reads anyway). Home's "you might like" |
| `coldStartTags` | A fixed list of niche tags across genres (`feed.rs`) |
| `cold` | Built with nothing known of the listener (nothing played, liked or followed): Home shows its first-run screen |
| `coldStartArtists` | On a first run, the artists of the starter tags' picks (6), to follow from the first-run screen |

Each reason carries its `evidence` (`taste::Evidence`): what the listener did with the seed artist or tag, as counts. The web says the strongest true one ("Similar to Clairo. You've played them 14 times."; a follow, then likes, plays, saves, saved albums, Last.fm history), never "an artist you like" of one only played.

**Where a pick plays** (`feed::check_picks`): after a build, never before Home is served, each pick shown is checked, one at a time. Soulseek is asked first (`Transfers::song_available`: a song with known sources answers at once, a remembered answer too, otherwise one search in the search budget's lowest lane, kept 7 days if found and 1 if not). YouTube is asked unless it is off (`Youtube::available`: a remembered match or answer at once, otherwise one flat video search, each result judged by playback's own rule, with no extraction; kept 30 days if found and 1 if not; one check at a time, started only while no other yt-dlp run is in flight). The pick then carries `avail` (`soulseek`, `youtube`, or both), or `noSource` when every source checked has none (Home: "Search again"), announced one by one as `feed.avail`. A pick carried over or shown again costs nothing. Measured on six real songs: 3.9 s and one request a song for the YouTube check, against 7.7 s and two (a YouTube Music search and an extraction) for playback's own match.

The four pick pools:

| Pool | Reason | via |
|---|---|---|
| Artist radio (medium, 0-60% popularity) from the seed artists (core and edge, above) by their MBID, else 6 songs of Deezer's radio by their Deezer id, minus the seed's own ([identity](#artist-identity)) | `like` (seed artist) | `ListenBrainz similar artists`, `Deezer radio` |
| Tag radio at 0-50% popularity for the seed tags (core and edge, above; 3 rotating cold-start tags when nothing is known) | `tag` | `ListenBrainz tags` |
| CF recommendations (only with `LULL_LISTENBRAINZ_USER`) | `listeners` ("listeners like you", no `ref`) | `ListenBrainz recommendations` |
| The first song the listener doesn't know (an original when there is one) of up to 3 fresh releases by followed or liked artists | `fresh` | `MusicBrainz releases` |

The UI words a reason by what it is and says "you follow" only for a follow (`ui.reasonText`).

`errors` names a shelf only when it came back empty because a source failed. Partial failures are logged.

## Radio (`GET /api/radio`)

- **Seed `tag`:** ListenBrainz tag radio.
- **Seed `artist`:** artist radio (15 similar artists, 4 recordings each).
- **Seed `track`:** its artist's radio plus its first specific tag's radio.
- **Finding the artist** (`radio.rs`): a credit is tried whole, then without its featured part, then artist by artist (split on "&", "," and "x"), then the featured artists ("Shawn Chrystopher feat. Cults & Ashly" → Shawn Chrystopher). The first name lull holds [ids](#artist-identity) for is the artist; a name alone never is. Deciding counts against the radio's time.
- **Without an MBID:** Deezer's radio of the artist's Deezer id. Without either, or when ListenBrainz has no artist radio for it, tag radio for the seed's first specific tag: a track's own tags, or those of the listener's songs by the artist.
- **When ListenBrainz gives nothing** (down, slower than 5 s, or empty), Last.fm fills the radio: `track.getSimilar` for a track seed; for an artist with an MBID (an artist seed, or a track without similar tracks), the artist's top tracks plus those of three similar artists asked by its MBID (further down Last.fm's list as `steer` rises); for an artist seed without one, `track.getSimilar` of three of the listener's songs by it; `tag.getTopTracks` for a tag. These songs name no release.
- **Then the saved library:** songs matching the artist (without its featured part) or the tag.
- **8 s at most** for the whole call. It fails only when no source gave tracks: 503 with the reason when none answered at all, else 404.

Umbrella genres (rock, pop, electronic, hip hop, indie…: `taste::UMBRELLA_TAGS`) are never dug into: they lead to charts, not niches. They stay in the taste profile.
- **`steer` (0 familiar … 1 adventurous, in quarter steps so answers are shared):**
  - picks the ListenBrainz mode: easy < 0.34 ≤ medium < 0.67 ≤ hard;
  - sets the popularity window: 0 is 40-100%, 1 is 0-50%;
  - nudges liked artists up (familiar) or down (adventurous).
- **Each call returns 12 tracks:** at most 2 per artist (except the seed artist), minus `exclude`, hidden and disliked artists.
- **Originals first:** a title whose brackets or " - " suffix say remix, live, edit, mix, version, dub, sped up, slowed, mashup, instrumental, demo or acoustic ("Talamak (Tom Lown Remix)", "Drift (sped up)") only fills a batch after every original, unless the seed track is such a take.

## Pages

| Page | From |
|---|---|
| **Album** | An album iTunes or Deezer found (search remembers its `collectionId` / album id in `discovery_album_sources`): that catalog's tracklist, one call (about 0.1-0.5 s from here); MusicBrainz then adds its MBID, tags, type and date in the background. Otherwise the MusicBrainz release group (by remembered MBID, else search by artist and title, version tags ignored) and the earliest official release's tracklist: two calls behind the shared 1.1 s pace. Without a match, the catalog's tracks of that album. **The page answers within 3 s:** a tracklist not in by then is still fetched and remembered, the page answers with the tracks lull has (`partial: true`, or `errors.tracks` when it failed the last time), and `album.updated` follows |
| **Artist** | See [Artist pages](#artist-pages) |
| **Tag** | MusicBrainz releases tagged it in the last 90 days; Last.fm description (without its "Read more on Last.fm" tail), top artists (with their Deezer photos) and similar tags. Without Last.fm: artists from tag radio, and related tags from tag co-occurrence on the releases. 404 when no source knows the tag (and none failed) |

A section whose source failed is empty and named in `errors`. **Artist and tag pages answer within 4 s:** their sections run at once, each in a task of its own. On a tag page, one not in by then is named in `errors` and, cached when it ends, shows on the next visit; an artist page names it in `pending` and announces `artist.updated` once every late section has ended. **Late work is bounded:** a section the page answered without arrives or fails within 90 s (`pages::LATE_LIMIT`, longer than any one source's own wait), so the `*.updated` push always follows. A section that failed the last time it ran is named in `errors` when it is late again (`pages::Unfinished`), not as coming once more, and failing again then is no news: no push. The artist's photo isn't a section the push waits for: one vetted after the answer arrives with `art.ready`. The UI reads a page that still names parts coming again when it is seen again and once after that bound, so a missed push never leaves a skeleton (web.md).

### Artist identity

Code: `src/artist/identity.rs`. **Identity is ids, never names.** Artist ids are name hashes, so namesakes share one record. By name, lull took Last.fm's MBID for "DOUDOU" (Doudou Masta, French hip hop) for the Chinese group whose song the listener played, and BIJI's tags from Last.fm's page for every "Biji" (kurdish, iraq): Last.fm's artist pages are per name, whatever MBID they are asked by. So an artist is the set of exact ids lull holds for it, at most one per source: Apple's artistId, Deezer's artist id, its MBID (`discovery_artist_ids`, each with its evidence). An id enters only from an exact reference:
- **The listener's songs:** a played or liked song's own catalog artist, read from the tracklist of the catalog album lull found it on (`Song::artist_source`; iTunes's from what searches leave spare, waiting its turn), and the library's MBID for a saved artist (Jellyfin's tags, `Library::artists`).
- **Links:** MusicBrainz's URL relationships, either way and to exactly one artist: a catalog id to an MBID (`/ws/2/url?resource=…&inc=artist-rels`: Apple's page on every storefront, in the one call (MusicBrainz keeps the storefront an editor linked: BIJI's is the UK one, whatever the listener's), its old iTunes form, Deezer's), an MBID to its Apple and Deezer artists (`artist_links`).
- **Credits that carry ids:** an ISRC of such a song (`/ws/2/isrc/<isrc>`): a recording credited to one artist alone, or the member whose MBID links to one of the artist's catalog ids ("BIJI & Mira Vale" is matched by id, never by name).
- **Recommenders' own ids** (`identity::offer`): ListenBrainz's recording and fresh-release artists (a credit of one artist), Deezer's related artists and radio, Last.fm's similar artists only with an MBID. A recommender that gives only a name gives nothing.
- **Search rows' own ids** (`Store::artist_sources`, from search's results): a catalog's artist row (Deezer's id), a song row's artist (Apple's artistId, Deezer's artist id). So an artist opened from search has its discography.

Precedence, so a name never decides: an artist the listener has songs of (or saved files) takes only the ids those give and their links; when they prove nothing, it holds nothing, and a recommender's or search row's id never joins it. A recommender or search row seeds an artist lull holds no id for (search's only before the listener's evidence was ever weighed; it replaces the seed); another source joins it only through a link. Two different ids of one source cancel each other. No name is compared anywhere, and nothing is scored.

Each feature uses whichever id serves it; one is missing only when no source holds an id for it:
- **discography:** iTunes's artist lookup by the Apple id, Deezer's albums by the Deezer id (where Deezer is open), MusicBrainz's release groups by the MBID;
- **similar artists:** ListenBrainz by the MBID, else Deezer's related artists by the Deezer id;
- **photo:** Wikidata by the MBID, else Deezer's picture of the Deezer id;
- **radio and picks:** ListenBrainz's artist radio by the MBID, else Deezer's radio by the Deezer id;
- **tags:** MusicBrainz's by the MBID; Last.fm's artist tags never count. Songs get their own: Last.fm's `track.getTopTags` by artist and title, for a listener's song the catalog has none for.

Kept in `discovery_artist_ids`, the only place an artist's ids live: an MBID that comes with a name (a search row, a Last.fm profile, a lull link) isn't kept, an artist without an MBID keeps no artist tags, and one whose MBID changes or goes loses its tags and its photo's verdict. The listener's evidence for an artist is weighed on first need (its page, a feed seed, a radio) and again after 7 days (`discovery_artist_checked`); a lookup that fails keeps nothing, so the next need asks again. A new rule version (`identity::RULE`) weighs every remembered artist the listener has songs of once at start, then rebuilds the feed. Cost per such artist: its songs' albums' tracklists (cached a week), one MusicBrainz URL lookup, its MBID's links, an ISRC lookup only when those find no MBID; all MusicBrainz answers kept 30 days, through the shared pace.

### Artist pages

Code: `src/artist/` (`mod.rs` the page, `discography.rs`, `popular.rs`, `photo.rs`). Why: [ADR-0031](../adr/0031-artist-pages.md).

- **Who it is:** its [ids](#artist-identity), first (within the 4 s; ids decided later are kept for the next visit). Each section asks the source it has an id for; none is ever found by name.
- **Namesakes are told apart** by those ids (BIJI shares the name with another Biji on iTunes and on Deezer):
  - Last.fm's profile (by MBID when there is one) gives the bio and listeners; one that comes back with another MBID is another artist's and is left out. Its tags never count.
  - Popular songs are only songs a catalog lists for this artist (Last.fm pools every artist of a name).
  - A credit "leads" a release when the artist is its whole main part or its first artist ("BIJI & JUGGLERZ", not "Conducta & BIJI" or "CHAMOS feat. BIJI"); credited anywhere counts for songs. Similar artists that are credit strings ("Kalman Chodakiewitz, Eli Shiffman & Petite Meller" beside Petite Meller) are dropped.
- **Discography** (`discography.rs`): iTunes's artist lookup (`entity=album`, every release the store lists for the artist, its own when the collection's `artistId` is the artist's, plus the albums its songs are on that those miss: soundtracks, compilations, other artists' EPs; DJ mixes left out), Deezer's artist albums where it is open, and MusicBrainz's release groups (with credits). Merged by loose title (case, punctuation, "(Explicit)", "(Deluxe)" aside), within the artist's own releases or those it appears on, and within a year; iTunes, then Deezer, then MusicBrainz gives a release's identity (its tracklist and cover come from that catalog in one call; the explicit edition before the clean one), the others add the MBID, a more precise date, a cover. Grouped like Spotify: `albums`, `singles` (singles and EPs), `compilations`, `appearsOn` (any release the artist doesn't lead), newest first. Each is remembered with its catalog source, so its album page asks that catalog.
- **Popular** (`popular.rs`): Last.fm's `artist.getTopTracks` (30, with play and listener counts; takes of one song summed: "Boya" and "Boya (from Cyberpunk: Edgerunners 2)"), placed on the catalog's songs (Deezer's artist top where open, then the iTunes song lookup's order, which follows popularity), on the artist's own release when it has one (the single over the series soundtrack). The catalog's order fills the 10 rows; without Last.fm it is the order. With no catalog songs: Last.fm's titles alone, else ListenBrainz artist radio sorted by listens. `counts` carries Last.fm's numbers, `popularVia` the source.
- **Similar:** ListenBrainz by the MBID, else Deezer's related artists by the Deezer id (it answers where the catalog is closed); 12. Each one's id is offered as who it is (`identity::offer`).
- **Photos** (`photo.rs`, the artist and its similar artists, after the catalog): never an album cover posing as one. Deezer shows a cover for an artist without a photo (BIJI's "photo" was the Cyberpunk: Edgerunners 2 cover). So: Wikimedia Commons by way of Wikidata (P18 for the MBID); else Deezer's picture of the artist's own Deezer id (or the one Wikidata links its MBID to), unless a 64-bit difference hash of a 96 px copy is within 10 bits of one of the artist's covers (the discography's newest 8, Deezer radio's). With no covers to compare with, no photo. The verdict is kept in `discovery_artist_photos` (a photo 30 days, "none" 7); changing it drops the cached files and changes the `?v=` of `image`. **Only a vetted photo is advertised or served**, anywhere: the picture search records for an artist may be a cover. An artist a screen shows without a photo (`PUT /api/pictures/screen`, size 0) is queued. The best-placed are vetted first, 12 to a batch with one Wikidata query; one that left every screen is dropped (it is reported again when it comes back), and none is vetted twice at once. The artist page holds its own artist, which it vets against the discography's covers. Wikidata is asked about the artist's own MBID only; an artist whose MBID changes is vetted again. A photo found is announced (`art.ready` with `images: { artistId: image }`), so the page shows it without reading anything again. Without a photo, each design draws its own fallback.
- **Cost of a first visit:** MusicBrainz 2 calls (links, release groups; with only catalog ids, a URL lookup first), iTunes 2 (the artist's two lookups, from what search leaves spare; over the budget they wait their turn and the page shows them late, `pending`), Deezer 1 (related artists; +2 where open, + per similar artist its picture and radio), Last.fm 2 (profile, top tracks), Wikidata 1, and small picture fetches to vet Deezer pictures. Later visits are cached (see the table).

## Search (`GET /api/search`, ADR-0008)

Code: `src/search/` (`mod.rs` runs a query, `merge.rs` merges and ranks), `src/art.rs` (pictures).

- **The fast catalog is chosen per hub** (`search/catalog.rs`): Deezer's `/infos` is asked at start and daily (hourly after a failure).
  - Where Deezer is open (`open: true`, and until it has answered), Deezer answers tracks, albums and artists.
  - Where it is closed (as from the homelab: track and album searches come back empty with `total` above zero), iTunes Search answers tracks and albums, and Deezer only artists and photos.
  - The choice is the `catalog` status (`/health`, the status tray): "catalog: Deezer", or "catalog: iTunes (US) + Deezer artists, as Deezer isn't open in this country". It turns `warn` with the reason when a fast source fails.
- **Sources, side by side:**
  - Deezer: tracks (25), albums (15), artists (10), or only artists where it is closed;
  - iTunes, where Deezer is closed: songs (25), with the albums they are on (`Creep - EP` is the EP "Creep"), and albums by their own name (25), so a soundtrack or compilation is found although none of its songs is named like the query. "Various Artists" albums are compilations. Apple gives no counts, so its order stands in for popularity (albums on Deezer's album scale, 0.6 at most; albums only known from a song at half its popularity, or all of it when the song was found for its album's name: "Brain Damage" for "dark side of the moon"). Its 18 calls a minute are shared by everyone on the hub; over the budget a call waits its turn (`Budget::take_turn`): a search's songs first, then its albums (both may use the whole budget), the newest search before the words typed on the way, then artist pages' catalogs and share links (what leaves 6 for searches), then link imports. The album call was once one of the spare ones: waiting behind work nobody was looking at, it held back the songs it arrives with, and 5 of 63 searches measured 2026-11-04 answered without iTunes. A search waits within its source's 8 s, so a search that gets no turn shows `errors.itunes` and its run isn't kept; it never answers as if iTunes had found nothing (an answer without iTunes's songs was kept 10 minutes and shown to everyone searching those words). Album search failing leaves the songs and shows in `errors.itunes`;
  - saved songs through the `Library` seam (25);
  - MusicBrainz: **one** recording search per query, `(recording:(w) AND artist:(w)) OR recording:"w" OR artist:"w"` on the normalized words. Its release groups and credited artists become album and artist rows, one artist row per credited artist ("Radiohead & Portishead" is two). Bootlegs, broadcasts, interviews and spoken word don't count as releases, and a recording only on those is left out ("Interview CD", radio sessions). A song's album is its first official album or soundtrack (else EP or single, else a compilation or live release). Songs and artists that don't hold every typed word are dropped (the query is broad); albums holding most of them stay. A recording ranks by how often it was released (100 releases score 1: Radiohead's "Creep", on 130, which Apple lacks; a bootleg on 1 scores 0.15), its album by half that;

  - for a **submitted** query (`submitted=1`), MusicBrainz release groups by title too, paced after the recording search: albums Apple lacks and Bandcamp-only EPs are found by their name ("skronk of the times" → HAIRY BABIES' EP). Rows that don't hold most of the words are dropped. 100 are asked for (about 200 KB, one call), as dozens share a title ("Nevermind": 145) and MusicBrainz's order doesn't put the famous one first; each ranks by its release count (Nirvana's Nevermind 100, its namesakes 1 or 2), so it leads once it is in. Like the recording search, it arrives after the first answer and is kept a day; its rows go after the fast sources' on a repeat too, so the first search and the next show one list.

  - YouTube Music's search (`search/youtube_music.rs`), a gap-filler behind the catalog: one call per query (3+ characters) to the endpoint its web player reads, as YouTube publishes no API for it; about 0.5 s (median of 17, 2026-11-04), so it is a fast source and answers in the first answer. Measured on the 69 songs of the owner's likes, library and history: Apple's search finds 52; of the 17 it misses, YouTube Music finds 7 and MusicBrainz 13 (0.9 s median, refused at times), together 15. Its top result, when a song or an album, counts as YouTube Music's best answer ("nevermind": Nirvana's album, which Apple lacks); its listed songs carry their plays (2.3B for "Creep"), its listed albums their order. Only rows the catalog lacks are added, and listed ones go after the catalog's rows of the same fit (`merge::fill_gaps`); a song the catalog has takes YouTube Music's plays when higher, as the catalog's order is a guess, but only from the same recording: the same artist and title, and, as YouTube Music gives no lengths, the same take (`same_take`: "Shelter (Them Jeans drum edit)" is not "Shelter", which is a row of its own; a namesake's 42M plays rank only itself, and "hello" stays Adele's). It waits for the catalog's songs, so the catalog's rows keep their identity. Kept 10 minutes, at most 5 calls in 5 s lull-wide, a 4 s timeout; a failure is no rows and `errors.youtube-music`, never a failed run. Its answer's shape is YouTube's to change: the `youtube-music-contract` workflow reads it live daily and when the reader changes.

  - YouTube's own uploads, the last gap-filler: once the catalog's songs and YouTube Music have answered and no row holds every word typed, one YouTube video search (`Youtube::search_songs`, yt-dlp, seconds: after the first answer, kept a day). Each upload is read as a song and kept only when playback's judging takes it for that song (youtube.md#matching), so what search offers plays; a fan's "Title - Artist" is read the other way round when the other uploads put its title part first more often. Its songs holding every word are fillers, added only where nothing else has them ("clairo mount airy georgia": only fans uploaded it, as "Clairo - mount airy, georgia (Sub.Español)" and "mount airy, georgia - clairo").

  Last.fm's search is not a source: measured 2026-11-04, it added only covers with no release ("smells like teen spirit": 13 of 21 later rows), interviews and scrobbles' credit strings as artists ("Daft Punk Get Lucky"), nothing the catalogs and MusicBrainz missed.

  MusicBrainz is asked only for 3+ characters: MusicBrainz's one call a second is shared with every lull crate.
- **Timing:** the answer goes out once every fast source (Deezer, iTunes, the library) is in, or at 1.5 s, so its top result and order come from all of them (an answer at 400 ms with only Deezer's artists in led "blinding lights" with a small artist of that name, and shown rows are never reordered); but never before the catalog's songs (Deezer's, or iTunes's where Deezer is closed), whenever they come within their source's 8 s ("abbey road" answered at 1.5 s without iTunes led with an artist called Abbey Road and listed no album). From the homelab iTunes takes 0.6-1 s and Deezer's artist search about 0.6 s, measured 2026-11-01. The rest arrives as `search.more` with only rows not shown yet, plus entities of shown rows that gained facts (art, length). The log line `search answered` has the milliseconds.
- **Runs:** one per query (lowercase, single spaces) for 10 minutes, shared by every request in that time, so a repeat answers at once. A run where a source failed isn't reused.
- **One list per run** (`Board`): the first answer fixes the top result and the order from the fast sources; a slow source that answered before it (its answer kept from an earlier search: MusicBrainz's for a day) goes after those rows, as it would have come as `search.more` (`first_board`), so a search and its repeat show the same list ("nevermind" led with Nirvana's album only on the second search); each `search.more` adds its rows after the ones shown; every later answer for the run (another listener, the `submitted=1` call) is that list as it stands, never ranked anew. So two people searching the same words at about the same time see the same rows in the same order, and a late source never moves or drops a row.
- **Merging** (`merge.rs`): one song per shared ISRC, or per `ids::loose_artist` + `ids::loose_title` (feat., remaster, version, edit and live tags dropped; the same normalization Soulseek matches files with) with lengths within 3 s or unknown. A different ISRC doesn't keep rows apart: remasters and compilations get new ones. Albums merge by loose artist and title, artists by loose name.
- **A row keeps** the identity (and so the id) of its first source, and gains the others' facts. A song carries its artist's catalog id (`artist_source`: Apple's `artistId`, Deezer's artist id) and an artist the catalogs' ids for it (`sources`), only as the rows give them, never found by name; `SongHit::song` hands them on. Before the first answer, a saved copy lends its id, and a row without a release takes the first named one.
- **Ranking:** fit; then the song itself before its other versions; then popularity in tenths, then the listener's own (saved, liked, a followed artist), then exact popularity.
  - **Fit**, best first: exact (the title or name, an artist's songs and albums for their exact name, artist and title together, with any credited artist: "Daft Punk, Pharrell Williams & Nile Rodgers" is Daft Punk's; a title that begins with two or more words typed, as the listener is still typing: "smells like teen"; an artist's name and words of the title: "nirvana unplugged"); else every typed word starts a word of the row; else most of them (two or more, and two thirds of the words that count: numbers, and words of four letters or more, so "of" and "the" don't); else one word that counts ("lemon" of "米津玄師 lemon", whose artist is in another script); else loose. Words found only in a "(from …)" note count as most: "You Can't Run From Me (from Cyberpunk: Edgerunners Season 2)" is from the work typed, not that work.
  - **Typos and accents:** accents are folded on both sides ("beyonce" holds "Beyoncé"); a typed word of five letters or more may be one edit in five from a word of the row, two at most ("beyonse", "fleetwod", "elish"; a swap of neighbours is one edit: "indain"), and the whole query one in eight ("bohemian rapsody" is exactly "Bohemian Rhapsody"), so a short word never passes for another ("creep" isn't "creed"). Before, every misspelt query led with an album: "radiohed creep" with OK Computer, "fleetwod mac dreams" with a Greatest Hits.
  - **Other versions:** a song whose title or release has a bracket or " - " suffix naming another take (live, remix, mix, edit, version, acoustic, unplugged, instrumental, demo, karaoke, cover, "originally performed by", tribute, lullaby rendition, reprise, slowed, sped up, lo-fi, take, rehearsal; `merge::VERSION_TAGS`, the list radio's versions are told by too), never by its title's own words ("Live and Let Die"), or that is on a live release ("Live at Reading"), goes after the songs of the same fit, unless that word was typed ("hotel california live"; "unplugged" counts for live and acoustic). Fit compares the title without those tags, so "Creep (Acoustic)" is still exactly Radiohead's Creep. A merged row not shown yet takes the song's own naming over a live or remixed release's ("Dynamite" on BE, not on a concert album). Remasters and "feat." are the same recording.
  - **Well-known songs:** each further take of a song by its own artists in the answers (its album's deluxe edition, a best-of, a soundtrack, a live take, an official remix or instrumental; one per release, a single being its song) closes 15% of the gap between its popularity and full (`merge::known`): the catalogs' sign of a well-known song where they give no counts. "blinding lights" is The Weeknd's (on After Hours, The Highlights, remixes, an instrumental), not Teddy Swims's single, which iTunes listed first. Popularity is a log scale per source: Deezer rank (1e6 = 1), Deezer fans (1e7), Last.fm listeners (tracks 5e6, artists 1e7). Deezer albums have no count, so their order stands in. MusicBrainz rows have none.
- **Borrowed names:** an artist whose name holds another artist's and more ("Radiohead - Topic", "We Love Taylor Swift", "SZA with Kendrick Lamar") with under 1% of its audience (none known counts as none) isn't listed (`merge::borrowed`).
- **Famous near matches:** an artist with under a tenth of the fans or listeners of a confirmed artist that holds every typed word or is a typo away ranks at that artist's fit, so popularity decides ("mozart" is Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart before a pop act called Mozart; "radiohed" is Radiohead before a namesake MusicBrainz knows).
- **Titles and "the":** a leading "the" is ignored on either side, for titles as for names ("dark side of the moon" is exactly "The Dark Side of the Moon").
- **Top result:** the best of the first artist, album and track: fit, then the song itself (or its release) before another version ("Get Lucky", not the album of its Daft Punk Remix), then popularity, with artists and full-length albums (album, compilation) leaning +0.15, and EPs and singles +0.05 ("radiohed" is Radiohead, "creep" is the song, "cyberpunk edgerunners season 2" is the soundtrack "Cyberpunk: Edgerunners 2").
- **The query set** (`search/query_set.rs`): 34 queries, with what the listener meant by each (misspellings, partial titles, artist and title in either order, another script, versions (sped-up and instrumental takes typed or not), albums named like songs, words that are also bands), ranked on the fast sources' answers recorded for them (`fixtures/query-set.json`, 2026-11-04). On the ranking before these rules, 19 of the 32 first written led with the wrong thing. A change to ranking or matching keeps it passing; re-record when a case's catalogs have moved.
- **`search.catalog`** names the 50 best tracks once every source is in (at the answer when all were in by then).
**Deezer withholds its catalog where it isn't open.** From the homelab's IP, `/search/track` and `/search/album` answer `data: []` with `total` above zero, and `/infos` says `open: false`. Only `/search/artist` and lookups by id work. Should Deezer be searched for tracks there anyway (before `/infos` has answered), that is named in `errors.deezer` ("isn't open in this country") rather than taken as "nothing found".

## Pictures (`GET /api/catalog/art/{id}?size=96|256|512|1024|2048`)

Served by the hub's picture service (`lull_core::pictures`, [ADR-0033](../adr/0033-one-picture-service-driven-by-the-screen.md)), which library's `/api/art` and Subsonic share; discovery is its finder (`art::Covers`): where each picture comes from.

- **Where a picture comes from:**
  - An album's cover is the source search recorded (`catalog_albums.art`, a URL with `{size}`: Deezer, iTunes). Without one, it is looked up by name, one service a step, each in the picture service's lane for it:
    1. Last.fm `album.getInfo`;
    2. iTunes `entity=album`, from what searches leave spare (`Need::Background`);
    3. the Cover Art Archive by release-group MBID.
    
    Measured from the homelab on 41 of a listener's albums (2026-11-04), that order finds 90%. Last.fm finds 76% in 0.5 s, and its CDN serves the picture in 0.07 s; the archive takes 3 s a thumbnail. MusicBrainz's name search found nothing more and is not used. The source found is recorded (`catalog::found_album_art`), so every size and every later request reuses it. Last.fm's and iTunes' answers are kept 30 days.
  - An artist's picture is only a vetted photo (`discovery_artist_photos`, [Artist pages](#artist-pages)). The `catalog_artists.image` search records is never served.
  - A search playlist's picture is its own source, remembered a month.
- **Entities:**
  - Albums carry `art` (`api/catalog/art/<id>`, relative to the page; the UI adds `?size=`) when their source is known, or when they aren't saved and have a name to look one up by. So new releases and the songs an import named have covers without waiting for each song to be resolved. Saved albums without a source use library's `/api/art`.
  - Artists carry `image` (with `?v=`) for a vetted photo only.
  - Neither is given once the hub learnt lately (a day) that no source has one: the UI shows its placeholder at once instead of asking for a 404 on every load.
- **The order work runs in** is the screens' (`PUT /api/pictures/screen`, api.md):
  1. what plays: the playing song's album at 256, 512 and 1024, and the next 3 songs' at 256 and 512, from `device.state`; unsaved songs' albums here, saved ones' in library;
  2. what is visible;
  3. what is near;
  4. what a request waits for;
  
  within each, newest screen first, then top to bottom. Work no screen shows any more is dropped before it starts. An answer no longer fetches the pictures it names: what the page then shows is.
- **Fetching:** a request waits at most 3 s, then answers 404 "not yet" (`no-store`), and the work goes on. One job per picture: it fetches the largest size wanted while it waited, at the source's best for it, and scales every smaller served size from it. When it lands, `art.ready { keys }` tells the pages (batched over 250 ms), and they ask again. A failed step is tried again after 20 s, then 40 s, in the screen's place at the time.
- **Each source at its best for the size** (`lull_core::pictures::sized`):
  - the archive's smallest thumbnail that covers it (250 for 96 and 256, 500 for 512, 1200 for 1024), its original for 2048, and the original when a thumbnail is missing;
  - Last.fm's 174 px for 96, its 300 px for 256, its original above (its CDN serves no other sizes);
  - iTunes (`{size}x{size}bb`) and Commons (`?width=`) at the size, which they serve up to their original and never past it (checked 2026-11-03: iTunes answered 2048 px at 2048, and its 4000 px original at 5000);
  - Deezer at the size up to 1400 px. Its CDN answers up to 1900 px (403 above) whatever its master is, and 1400 px is the smallest master it takes from labels, so 1400 is never blown up.
  
  Every picture is scaled down to fit the size asked for (JPEG) and never up (`X-Image-Size` gives the real size). `size` defaults to 256.
- **Cache:** `<data dir>/catalog-art/<id>-<size>`, 30 days. "The source has none" is `<id>.none`, one for every size, for a day.

## Share links (`GET /api/share/{kind}/{id}`)

`share.rs`: a public link and cover for a track, album or artist, used by the share menu and as the desktop app's Discord Rich Presence art.
- **Songs and albums:** one iTunes Search call for `artist title` (`entity=song` or `album`, 10 results). The first listing by the same artist (or a credit including them) with the same title wins, else the same title loosely (`ids::loose_title`). The link is Odesli's page for that iTunes id: `https://song.link/i/<trackId>` or `https://album.link/i/<collectionId>`, which lists every streaming service.
- **Why not Odesli's API:** its keyless API answers 401 `PUBLIC_API_ACCESS_DEPRECATED` (checked 2026-11-02). Its pages still resolve an iTunes id, so lull builds the page URL and makes no Odesli call. `song.link/i/<collectionId>` and `artist.link/i/<artistId>` don't resolve; `album.link/i/<collectionId>` does.
- **Artists, and anything the store doesn't list:** the Last.fm page (`https://www.last.fm/music/<artist>[/_/<track> | /<album>]`), `source: "lastfm"`.
- **Artwork:** the store's cover at 600 px (`…/600x600bb.jpg`), else the public source the catalog knows for the album or artist (Deezer, iTunes, the Cover Art Archive) at 600 px, else `null`.
- **Cached** in `discovery_cache` (`share:<kind>:<id>`): store links 30 days, Last.fm fallbacks 1 day, so a release that reaches the store later gets its link. An expired answer is served when iTunes fails. One link is resolved at a time, so the same link asked for twice at once costs one call. iTunes calls share the 18-a-minute budget with search; over it the call waits its turn (after searches' songs), and only a store that fails answers 503.
- **`link`, what Copy link copies,** names the item and the public ids lull knows, never the hub's address or a token:
  - **a share host's page** (`share_host::link`, ADR-0034, share.md) once `share/hosts.json` names a canonical host: `https://<host>/1/song/<artist>/<title>?al=&aa=&len=&it=&ic=&dz=&mb=` (album and artist alike). `it` is the iTunes id behind `url`, `ic`/`dz` the album's catalog id, `mb` the album's MBID. Chat apps show it as a card;
  - until then, lull's open page (`https://tannhausercommons.github.io/lull/open`, `site/`, ADR-0032) followed by `#lull?` and what lull knows (`open::link`): `kind=song|album|artist`, `artist`, `title`, `album`, `by` (the album's artist when it differs), `length` (s), `mbid`, the album's catalog id (`deezer=` or `itunes=`), and `page=` (`url`). Browsers never send what follows `#`.
  
  Either page opens the listener's lull (in the browser, or the app) or offers `url`.

## Opening links (`GET /api/open?link=`)

`open.rs`: a link someone shared becomes this hub's own page, on any hub. Why this shape: [ADR-0032](../adr/0032-lull-links-are-public-pages-with-ids.md), [ADR-0034](../adr/0034-lull-links-are-cards-served-from-cloudflare.md).
- **Read:** a lull link's `#lull?` ids first (whatever page precedes them); a share host's `/1/` link (`share_host::read`: any `/1/` address on a host in `share/hosts.json`, and one shaped as a song, album or artist link on any other https host, so a self-run share host works too; a broken one is a 400); else song.link and album.link (`/i/<iTunes id>`), Apple Music (`?i=<song>` or the last path id), Deezer `track|album|artist/<id>`, MusicBrainz `release-group/<mbid>`, Last.fm `music/<artist>[/_/<song> | /<album>]` (names). Anything else is a 400 saying what lull reads. Playlist links aren't read here: the UI opens them as an outside playlist.
- **Asked:** an iTunes id with one `lookup` (this hub's store, then the US one; a month in the cache; it waits its turn of the budget as a search's songs do), a Deezer id by id (answers where Deezer is closed), a release group from MusicBrainz.
- **Recorded** like a search row (`store.tracks`, `store.albums`, `store.artists`), the album with its catalog source so its page lists the tracklist; a song named without an album (Last.fm) is looked for in the catalog first (`Catalog::find_song`). The same names give the same ids on every hub (`ids::TrackId::of`).

## Playlists in search

`search/playlists.rs`. For 3+ characters, search also asks for playlists people made: Deezer's `search/playlist` (15, about 0.4 s from the homelab: a fast source, in the first answer) and YouTube's search filtered to playlists through yt-dlp (`Youtube::search_playlists`, 12, a few seconds: usually a `search.more`). YouTube Music's own search lists the same community playlists, without names, so YouTube's stands for both. Both are keyless and kept a day (`dz:playlists:`, `yt:playlists:`), so the same words later get YouTube's in the first answer. There is no Spotify: its search needs a key.
- **Relevance first:** the title against the words typed (fit as album titles fit, but a title that is just the words counts as one holding every word, since anyone can name a playlist anything; a playlist holding less than most of the words is left out), then soundtrack wording ("soundtrack", "OST", "score", "songs from", "songs used", "list of songs", "tracklist"…).
- **Then size and popularity:** 0.4 × the site's own order (1/(1 + place/3)) + 0.3 × size (√(songs/100)) + 0.3 × following (log fans, 100 000 full); unknown size or following counts 0.4 / 0.3. YouTube's search gives neither, so its order stands in for them. Deezer's fans come only with a playlist's whole page (seconds for a long one), so an answer carries the fans already known and the first 4 unknown are fetched after it (inside Deezer's window, kept a week), for the next search.
- **Top result:** a query names a film, show or game when two or more playlists holding every word say soundtrack. Then the album holding every word that says soundtrack takes the top ("cyberpunk edgerunners 2" is still its OST album), a full-length one before a single ("interstellar soundtrack" is Hans Zimmer's, not a single called "Interstellar Soundtrack"), and without one, the best such playlist (`top.kind: playlist`; "person of interest"). A work named after a song is the song when the soundtrack is the song's artist's and the song fits exactly ("bohemian rhapsody" is Queen's song, not the film's soundtrack), and words naming a song's artist and title name the song, whatever the playlists say ("lose yourself eminem", though 8 Mile's playlists say soundtrack). Otherwise search's own top stands. Playlists arriving later (`search.more`) never change the top.
- **Pictures** go through `/api/catalog/art/pls-<link in hex>`, their source kept a month in `discovery_cache`, so the CSP stays same-origin.
- Opening one previews it without importing; collection.md#outside-playlists.

## Matching another service's songs (the `Catalog` seam)

Collection's playlist link imports ask `Catalog::find_song` for each song a Spotify, Deezer, YouTube or SoundCloud playlist names (`search/matching.rs`).
- **Order:** the library's copy (search's library half, `artist title`); then Deezer's `track/isrc:<isrc>` when the source gives one (it answers in every country, within Deezer's 50 per 5 s, so an ISRC playlist matches in seconds); then by name: Deezer `search/track` (10) where it is open, else iTunes `entity=song` (10). An ISRC match takes Deezer's album naming, which can differ from iTunes search's (another id for the same song).
- **Same song:** search's rule (`merge::same_take`: a shared ISRC, or loose artist and title with lengths within 3 s, and where a length is missing the same version tags, so a line "Shelter (Them Jeans drum edit)" never finds the plain "Shelter", rule 3), tried with the whole credit and each name in it (`radio::credited_names`, the reading radio uses: "Daft Punk, Pharrell Williams", "A feat. B"), so a credit with a comma in a name ("Tyler, The Creator") still matches as a whole. The first hit in the catalog's order that is the same song wins.
- **Names in another script** can't be compared (ミドリ in a Spotify playlist, "Midori" in iTunes): when the artist or the title is in Latin letters on one side and not on the other, the catalog's top hit for the query counts if both lengths are known and within 3 s and the names in one script agree (`across_scripts`). No transliteration. Search's own dedupe (`same_song`) is unchanged.
- **The rule's version** is `seams::FIND_SONG_RULE`; bump it with any change to these rules, so collection asks again about songs an older rule matched to nothing.
- **A library search that fails** fails the lookup (the import counts it as not looked up), rather than matching without the library.
- **Recorded** like a search row (track, album with its cover, catalog source), so the id resolves and the album page opens.
- **Limits:** iTunes match lookups spend only the calls the listener's searches leave spare (12 of 18 a minute), waiting for one rather than skipping, and are kept 7 days (`it:match:`). Deezer name lookups are kept a day, ISRC lookups 30 days, inside Deezer's shared window.

## Bare songs

`search/resolve.rs`, `lull_core::catalog` (`remember_named`, `enrich`). A song recorded from another source's text (an imported line, a playlist's song with no match, a Last.fm-only result, file tags) is bare: no album lull has a picture for. Every crate records such songs one way (`catalog::remember_named`: a richer record is kept, the source's ISRC is kept as a hint, the source's own picture becomes its cover), and nobody resolves them when recording.
- **The UI asks:** `ui.cover` (web) collects every song it draws that isn't saved and has no pictured album, and posts them to `POST /api/catalog/resolve` (each once a session). So every path is covered, including ones added later.
- **At once:** songs lull can picture already (a known album cover, a source's own picture, a resolved song) come back with their album pictured.
- **In the background:** a bare song due a lookup (never tried, or missed a day ago, `catalog_resolutions`) is found as `Catalog::find_song` finds songs (the library, Deezer by ISRC, then the catalog by name: case, "feat." and remaster tags aside, a remix is another song), one at a time, in the order its cover stands on the listener's screens (`Pictures::rank`); the rest oldest first. Its cover doesn't wait for this: its album's is looked up by name ([Pictures](#pictures)). A match fills the bare record in place (`catalog::enrich`: the catalog's spelling, album and cover, the same id, so playlists, likes and plays naming it change too); batches go out as `catalog.tracks`, a second apart.
- **Old imports** are covered the same way: their songs are resolved the first time they are shown.

## Ids

Album and artist ids are hashes (`lull_core::ids`), so discovery remembers every album and artist it shows (`discovery_albums`, `discovery_artists`), and every album's artist with it (with the MBID the release names), so an album's artist link resolves. An unknown id falls back to the catalog: the first track with that `albumId` / `artistId`. Tracks go to the catalog. An id the catalog already has keeps every fact it has, because another crate may have added file facts. Discovery only fills its gaps: year, number, disc, length and tags.
